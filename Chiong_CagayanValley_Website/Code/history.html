Our History & Culture

A Brief History

People have lived in Cagayan Valley for a very long time. In 2007, archaeologists found a foot bone in Callao Cave that is about 67,000 years old, which makes it the oldest known human fossil in the Philippines. The valley has been home to the Ibanag, Itawis, Gaddang, Ivatan, and Isneg peoples.

The Cagayan River was the main road of the valley for centuries. Spanish missionaries and soldiers arrived in the 1500s and founded towns along the river, including Tuguegarao, which later became the regional center. Many old churches and bridges from that time are still standing.

Today the region is known for farming, especially rice, corn, and tobacco, and for tourism in the north. In Batanes, the Ivatan kept their stone-house traditions to live with the wind and the sea.

Cultural Highlights

Festivals

  • Bambanti Festival (Isabela): a celebration of the scarecrow and the harvest.
  • Pavvurulun Afi Festival (Tuguegarao, Cagayan): honors the city's culture with street dancing.
  • Ammungan Festival (Nueva Vizcaya): a gathering of the province's many cultures.

Traditional Crafts and Foods

  • Vakul and kanayi: Ivatan head and back covers made from palm fibers.
  • Pancit batil patong: noodles topped with egg, meat, and vegetables.
  • Tuguegarao longganisa: garlicky sausage with a bit of sourness.
  • Uvud balls: a Batanes dish made from banana stem.
